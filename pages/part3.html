3. Years to Understand

Finished: 2023 Published: March 26, 2026

I tried to give someone something
they had never really been given before.

I just didn't realize
what it would cost me
to keep giving it.

I tried to love the hurt out of someone.
To show them what safety could feel like.
To prove that not everyone leaves.

So I stayed.

Long after it stopped feeling mutual.
Long after I started feeling alone beside them.

Through the silence.
Through the chaos.
Through the distance.
Through the slow understanding
that love was only moving
in one direction.

I kept believing
that if I loved a little deeper,
if I stayed a little longer,
if I carried a little more…

maybe one day it would meet me halfway.

And somewhere along the way
I finally stopped and realized

it had been years.

And I kept telling myself
it would be worth it.

That I would be worth it.

Because I should've been.

And somewhere in that time
I slowly disappeared inside the effort.

It took me years
to understand why.

Years
of believing love could heal
what wasn't ready to be faced.

Years
of hoping tomorrow
would feel different.

Years
of trying to give them something
they weren't ready to receive.

But I learned something the hard way.

Hurt doesn't disappear
just because love arrives.

And love can't do the work
that someone refuses to face.

I kept pouring pieces of myself out,
thinking patience might soften their walls…
thinking effort might quiet the pain they carried.

Somewhere along the way
I convinced myself
that if I loved hard enough
I could carry both of our healing.

The truth is

I wasn't loving them wrong.
I was loving them
more than they were willing
to be loved.

And this is where
I hold myself accountable.

Because it was never my place
to fix what they wouldn't touch.

It wasn't their fault
that they couldn't receive it.

And it wasn't my failure
that I offered it.

The lesson was simply this:

You can't love the hurt
out of someone
who is still holding onto it.

So I let go.

Not out of anger.
Not out of bitterness.

But out of acceptance.


And it wasn't until later
that I began to understand something.

When you fight that hard
to reclaim your sanity…
to find your peace again…

your perspective changes.

If you truly understood
how hard it is
to regain your sanity
and peace,
you would understand
why I now have boundaries.

Because healing is a choice.

And the only choice
we can truly make
is our own.

Maybe that's the real gift
I found in all of this…

The love I kept trying to give away
was never wasted.

It just kept pouring into someone
who didn't know how to hold it.

For years
I gave it freely.

Now, for the first time,
I'm finally learning
how to give it back to myself.

And maybe one day
they'll look back
and understand.

I wasn't the one
who got away.

I was the one who tried.
The one who stayed.
The one who cared.

But because I finally learned
that the love I kept trying to give them
was the love
I should have been giving myself
all along…

I understand now.

Some lessons
only arrive after you've given
everything you had to give.

And some versions of us
only exist once.

The version of me
who loved like that…

will never exist again.
